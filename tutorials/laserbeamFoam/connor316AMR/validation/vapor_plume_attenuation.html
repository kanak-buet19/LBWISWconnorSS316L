Vapor Plume Beer–Lambert Attenuation

src/laserHeatSource/laserRayParticle.C + laserHeatSource.C  ·  Yang et al. 2026 IJHMT, Eq. 31–35
Physical model — ray cross-section
① Domain-integrated vapour pressure (once per timestep)
$$p_{\text{total}} = \sum_{\text{cells}} V_c \cdot p_{\text{amb}} \cdot \exp\!\left[\frac{L_v}{R_{\text{sp}}} \left(\frac{1}{T_{\text{vap}}} - \frac{1}{T_c}\right)\right] \quad [\text{Pa·m}^3]$$

Clausius–Clapeyron saturation vapour pressure summed over the whole domain. $R_{\text{sp}} = R/M_m$ (specific gas constant).

Physically: measures how much vapour has evaporated across the entire domain.

pVaporMax $= 8\times10^{-8}$ Pa·m³ — reference level at which $\alpha$ saturates to its maximum value. Decrease to make attenuation kick in earlier.

② Plume attenuation coefficient α (once per timestep)
$$\alpha = \mu_{\alpha,\max} \cdot \min\!\left(\frac{p_{\text{total}}}{p_{\text{vapor,max}}},\; 1\right) \quad [\text{m}^{-1}]$$

Linearly ramps from $0$ to muAlphaMax $= 310\ \text{m}^{-1}$ as vapour builds up, then saturates.

$\alpha = 0$: plume transparent.   $\alpha = 310\ \text{m}^{-1}$ with $s_\text{eff}=1\ \text{mm}$: $P_\text{out}/P_\text{in} = e^{-0.31} \approx 0.73$ (27 % power lost to plume).

③ Effective plume path length s_eff (per ray, per hit)
$$s_{\text{eff}} = \begin{cases} s_{\text{gas}} + H_{\text{plume}}\!\left(1 - \min\!\left(\dfrac{u_{\text{scan}}}{u_{\text{max}}},1\right)\right) & \text{first bounce (ambient → surface)} \\[8pt] s_{\text{gas}} & \text{subsequent bounces (inside keyhole)} \end{cases}$$

$s_{\text{gas}}$ = actual gas-path length the ray has travelled in gas cells since its last reflection (accumulated in laserRayParticle.C, reset to 0 after each bounce).

H_plume $= 1\ \text{mm}$ — virtual extra column of vapour above the simulation surface, only added for the first ray hit.

uMax $= 1.5\ \text{m/s}$ — scan speed above which motion sweeps the plume aside, reducing $H_{\text{plume}}$ contribution to zero. At $u_{\text{scan}} = 0$ (stationary spot): full $H_{\text{plume}}$ applies.

④ Ray power after plume — Beer–Lambert (applied before Fresnel absorption)
$$P_{\text{out}} = P_{\text{in}} \cdot \exp\!\bigl(-\alpha \cdot s_{\text{eff}}\bigr)$$

Applied at every interface hit. Attenuated power then enters Fresnel absorption.

$s_{\text{gas}}$ resets to $0$ after each reflection, so multi-bounce rays inside the keyhole only accumulate their in-keyhole gas path, not the full above-surface path.
